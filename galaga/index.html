How to Play

  • Move — drag anywhere on the screen. Your ship follows your thumb, so you can keep it out from under your finger.
  • Shoot — auto-fire is on by default. Turn it off to tap-to-fire, or use Space and arrow keys on a desktop.
  • Barrel roll — tap the pad bottom-right (or Shift/C). You cannot be shot down mid-roll. It is your only defence, and the best place to put a patron's boon.
  • Patrons — five of them, each doing one visible thing: Vulcan burns, Tempest arcs to a second raider, Hunter crits, Bulwark knocks fire out of the air, Siren scrambles. A boon fits a patron to one of your verbs — Cannon, Wing, Bomb, Hull or Roll — and each verb holds one patron at a time, so taking a new boon replaces what was there.
  • Sectors — after each wave you choose where to go next, and the reward is shown before you commit: a named patron's boon, a repair, munitions, a salvage cache, or contested space that is harder but pays double.
  • Formations — clear every raider in the wave to advance. Diving enemies are worth double.
  • Ships — you start with three in reserve and can never hold more than five. At five, extra lives stop dropping and pay points instead.
  • Losses carry — dying sweeps only the shots right on top of you, not the whole screen, and each ship lost costs a reroll, then a card off your next refit table. You get one emergency hull a run, and only if a boss would otherwise be met on your last ship.
  • Heat — linger in a wave and the raiders start diving and firing harder. The gauge under the wave number shows the pressure building.
  • Conditions — most waves from 3 on are flown under a modifier: armoured, frenzy, marksmen, barrage or volatile. It is announced before the wave.
  • Threat levels — clear the campaign and a harder tier unlocks on the menu. Each stacks on the last.
  • Combo — kills chain while the bar under your score is draining. Every five kills adds a multiplier, up to ×8. Going quiet drops it, and so does getting hit.
  • Bosses — waves 4, 8 and 12 end in a boss. Watch for the red charge glow: that means a beam is about to fire.
  • Weak points — every boss has a glowing core. Hits there do double damage, and it beats faster as its armour fails.
  • Loadout — every run opens with two free picks from three cards, before wave 1.
  • Refit — beat a boss and you get two more picks. Cards are permanent upgrades that stack for the rest of the run; rarer ones are stronger, and pacts trade a real drawback for a bigger payoff. Tap a card or press 1-3, and spend a reroll if you dislike the draw. Pacts only turn up after the first boss.
  • Elites — raiders ringed in gold take three times the punishment, are worth three times the score, and always drop a power-up. Every wave has at least one.
  • Scrap — every run banks scrap from score and, more heavily, from how deep you got, with a bounty for every boss you put down. Spend it in the Hangar on permanent perks that carry into all future runs. The best perks stay locked until a run has actually reached that far.
  • Capture — gold command craft sometimes drop into a hover and open a tractor beam. Fly into it and your ship is taken: you lose it, and it joins the formation under its captor. Destroy that captor and the fighter flies home and docks alongside you as a dual fighter — twin hulls, twin guns. Take a hit as a pair and you lose the second hull instead of a life.
  • Challenging stages — before each boss, a wave that does not shoot back and cannot touch you. Clear all sixteen for a perfect bonus.
  • When you lose a ship — the action drops into slow motion and names what destroyed you, so you can see the pattern that caught you out.
  • Pause — the pause screen lists the cards you are carrying, and toggles auto-fire, sound and rumble.
  • Power-ups — scarce on purpose. Elites always leave one; beyond that only red command craft and armoured gun platforms roll for a drop, and they rarely do. Losing a ship costs you whatever you were carrying.
